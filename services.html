Unlisted shares are equity securities of companies that are legally incorporated and compliant under the Companies Act, but whose shares are not actively traded on public stock exchanges such as the NSE or BSE. These include high-growth pre-IPO unicorns, established subsidiaries, financial market infrastructure corporations (e.g. NCDEX, PXIL), and mature unquoted enterprises.

Unlike listed stocks which fluctuate continuously due to day-to-day retail speculation, unlisted equities are transacted through compliant private off-market transfers, enabling institutional and discerning retail investors to participate in fundamental business expansion before public market debut.

Prices in the unlisted market are determined by market demand and supply dynamics, institutional block deals, recent private placement funding rounds, and corporate balance sheet fundamentals (such as EBITDA margins, PE multiple benchmarking, revenue growth, and net asset value per share).

As per SEBI guidelines, unlisted share prices are not speculative tick-by-tick quotes. Meera Associates verifies institutional counterparty order books to provide real-time, executable price quotes directly to clients upon inquiry.

Unlike listed stocks where you can buy a single share, unlisted shares are transacted in minimum lot sizes to ensure commercial viability and depository transfer efficiency. Typical lot sizes generally start from ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 depending on the company, per-share valuation, and institutional lot availability.

Meera Associates accommodates customized lot sizes tailored for retail investors, HNIs, family offices, and corporate treasury allocations.

Yes, absolutely. An unlisted share represents legitimate legal ownership in the company. As an equity shareholder holding shares in electronic Demat form, you are entitled to all statutory benefits declared by the company, including:

  • Dividend Payments: Directly credited by the company's Registrar and Transfer Agent (RTA) to your bank account linked to your Demat.
  • Bonus Issues & Stock Splits: Additional shares are automatically credited into your Demat account on the corporate record date.
  • Rights Issues: Entitlement to apply for new shares at discounted rates as an existing shareholder.
  • Annual General Meetings (AGM): Right to receive corporate annual reports and vote on key resolutions.

When an unlisted company files its Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) and executes an Initial Public Offering (IPO), your existing unlisted Demat shares automatically transition into publicly traded listed shares under the same International Securities Identification Number (ISIN) or a newly designated listed ISIN.

Once the shares list on the NSE or BSE, they reflect directly inside your standard broker trading terminal (e.g. Zerodha, Groww, Angel One, Upstox) and can be traded on the exchange after the statutory pre-IPO lock-in expires.

Under SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, all equity shares held by non-promoters prior to the IPO are subject to a mandatory 6-month lock-in period from the date of commercial listing (reduced from the previous 1-year mandate).

During this 6-month window, the shares remain safe inside your Demat account but cannot be traded on the stock exchange terminal. Once the 6-month lock-in elapses, the depository removes the freeze, and the shares can be sold freely in the open secondary market at prevailing listed prices.

Any Indian resident or entity can invest. There is no legal restriction barring retail investors from acquiring unlisted shares. All you need is an active PAN card, standard KYC documents, and an operational NSDL or CDSL Demat account.

An off-market transfer is a direct electronic transfer of securities between two Demat accounts through national depositories (NSDL / CDSL) without routing through a public stock exchange matching engine. It can be executed via:

  • Online e-DIS: Using CDSL Easiest or NSDL SPEED-e portals without physical paper slips.
  • Physical DIS: Submitting a signed Delivery Instruction Slip to your broker/bank DP.

Both methods are fully recognized by SEBI and depositories with complete audit trails, government stamp duty receipt, and SMS/Email verification directly from NSDL/CDSL.

To transfer shares online from an NSDL Demat account:

  1. Visit the official NSDL portal (eservices.nsdl.com) and register for IDEAS / SPEED-e under Password / OTP mode.
  2. Add Meera Associates' designated Demat account as an approved pre-notified beneficiary.
  3. Once the DP verifies the beneficiary (~30 mins to 4 hours), select the company ISIN, enter quantity, and authorize the transfer using OTP authentication.

CDSL Demat holders (Zerodha, Groww, Angel One, Upstox, etc.) must register for CDSL Easiest (not "easi", which is view-only):

  1. Visit web.cdslindia.com/myeasi/Registration/EasiestRegistration.
  2. Enter your 16-digit BO ID and mobile OTP. Choose "Trusted Account (PIN)" mode (requires no expensive digital token).
  3. Enter Meera Associates' designated 16-digit CDSL clearing BO ID as your trusted account.
  4. Set your 8-digit alphanumeric Easiest PIN to authorize transfers paperlessly.

Read our step-by-step walkthrough: CDSL Easiest Registration & Beneficiary Guide ↗.

CDSL TPIN (6-digit numeric): Used inside your broker's mobile trading app (e.g. Zerodha Kite or Groww) for authorizing on-market listed stock sales.

CDSL Easiest PIN (8-digit alphanumeric): A separate security PIN created specifically inside the official CDSL Easiest web portal for authorizing off-market unlisted share transfers to trusted beneficiaries. When transferring unlisted shares on Easiest, your 6-digit broker TPIN will not work — you must enter your 8-digit Easiest PIN.

Approval timelines vary slightly between depositories:

  • NSDL SPEED-e: Beneficiary activation typically takes 30 minutes to 4 hours, with instant OTP validation on several participating DPs.
  • CDSL Easiest: CDSL mandates a standard security cooling period of 24 to 48 business hours for your broker/DP to approve newly added trusted BO IDs.

A Client Master List (CML) (also known as Client Master Report or CMR) is the official certificate issued by your DP confirming your 16-digit Demat ID, account status, registered name, PAN, bank account details, and signature registration.

To prevent fraudulent third-party transfers and ensure full SEBI compliance, depositories require a CML copy with a verified broker digital or physical seal to validate the authenticity of the buyer or seller before executing off-market transfers.

As per SEBI mandates, physical share certificates cannot be transferred or traded in paper form and must be dematerialized. The process involves:

  1. Open an active Demat account matching the exact name on the paper share certificate.
  2. Fill out and sign a Dematerialization Request Form (DRF) in triplicate.
  3. Surrender the original physical share certificates (defaced with "Surrendered for Dematerialisation") to your DP.
  4. The DP generates a Demat Request Number (DRN) and sends the physical certificates to the company's Registrar and Transfer Agent (RTA) for electronic confirmation and credit into your Demat.

Meera Associates provides complete documentation, tracking, and liaison support with all major RTAs (KFintech, Link Intime, Bigshare, etc.).

Standard documentation required includes:

  • Original Physical Share Certificates.
  • Duly filled and signed Demat Request Form (DRF) with DRN slip.
  • Self-attested copy of PAN card and Aadhaar card of all joint holders.
  • Client Master List (CML) of the target Demat account.
  • Form ISR-1 (Request for registering PAN, KYC details, bank mandate) and Form ISR-2 (Confirmation of signature by banker), as mandated by SEBI.

Name discrepancies (e.g. initials vs. full name, maiden name vs. married name, minor spelling errors) are common in vintage paper shares. RTAs reject demat requests if names do not match PAN databases exactly.

Meera Associates resolves these hurdles by drafting legally compliant gazette notifications, notarized affidavits, joint holder declarations, and SEBI Form ISR-1 submissions to harmonize company records with your active Demat credentials.

If physical share certificates are lost, you must apply for Duplicate Share Issuance before they can be dematerialized. The procedure involves:

  • Filing a Police First Information Report (FIR) or Non-Cognizable Report (NCR) for the lost certificates with folio numbers and distinctive numbers.
  • Publishing a public loss notice in English and regional vernacular newspapers (if total market valuation exceeds statutory thresholds).
  • Executing an Indemnity Bond (Form ISR-4) and Affidavit on non-judicial stamp paper.
  • Submitting documents to the company RTA to receive a Letter of Confirmation (LOC), which is then directly converted into Demat.

Meera Associates handles the end-to-end duplicate share recovery process on your behalf.

When a registered shareholder passes away, the process of transferring shares to surviving joint holders or legal heirs is known as Transmission:

  • If Joint Holders Exist: Surviving holder submits a notarized Death Certificate and Form ISR-1 to delete the deceased name.
  • If Sole Holder with Registered Nominee: Nominee submits notarized Death Certificate and KYC to claim shares.
  • If Sole Holder without Nominee: Legal heirs submit Succession Certificate / Probate of Will / Letter of Administration, or for claims below statutory limits, a Legal Heir Certificate, No Objection Certificates (NOC) from other heirs, and an Indemnity Bond.

Our senior legal team assists families in navigating succession paperwork seamlessly.

Under Section 124(6) of the Companies Act, 2013, if a shareholder does not claim their dividend for 7 consecutive years, the company is legally mandated to transfer both the unclaimed dividend amount AND the underlying equity shares to the IEPF Authority under the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA), Government of India.

Once transferred, the shares no longer appear in company share registers and cannot be sold until formally claimed back from the IEPF Authority.

Meera Associates provides complete, end-to-end IEPF recovery facilitation:

  • Asset Tracing: Identifying transferred folios, unpaid dividend amounts, and corporate entitlement histories across corporate registers.
  • Documentation & Affidavits: Drafting indemnity bonds, advance stamped receipts, and verification affidavits on required stamp paper denominations.
  • Digital Filing: Preparing and uploading e-Form IEPF-5 on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) portal.
  • Nodal Officer Liaison: Coordinating physical verification files with the respective company Nodal Officer and RTA.
  • Tracking to Demat Credit: Monitoring the claim until shares are released from the IEPF Authority and credited directly into your active Demat account.

Key documentation required for Form IEPF-5 includes:

  • Printout of submitted e-Form IEPF-5 and SRN acknowledgment Challan.
  • Original Advance Stamped Receipt signed by the claimant with revenue stamp.
  • Original Indemnity Bond executed on non-judicial stamp paper.
  • Original Physical Share Certificates (if shares were held physically) or CML statement.
  • Self-attested copy of Aadhaar Card, PAN Card, and Passport (for NRIs).
  • Original Cancelled Cheque and Bank Verification Letter (attested by bank manager).
  • Death Certificate, Legal Heir Certificate, or Succession Certificate (for inheritance claims).

The standard statutory timeline for IEPF recovery ranges from 4 to 8 months, depending on:

  • Company Verification: Company Nodal Officer has 30 days to verify physical documents and submit an e-Verification report to the MCA.
  • IEPF Authority Sanction: The government authority scrutinizes the file and approves electronic release of shares and refund of dividend funds directly to the claimant's bank account via PFMS (Public Financial Management System).

Meera Associates tracks each file weekly to eliminate bureaucratic delays and expedite approvals.

Yes, 100% legal. The transfer of unlisted shares is expressly governed and recognized by the Companies Act, 2013, the Depositories Act, 1996, and SEBI regulations. In full compliance with SEBI rules, all unlisted equity transfers are executed in electronic Demat mode via national depositories (NSDL / CDSL) between consenting parties.

Meera Associates executes strictly compliant off-market transactions backed by PAN verification, Client Master List (CML) validation, and statutory stamp duty payment.

Under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 (amended with uniform national rates), stamp duty @ 0.015% (15 paise per ₹1,000) of the total consideration value is legally mandatory on all off-market share transfers.

Stamp duty can be calculated and paid online via the official NSDL or CDSL stamp duty payment gateways before the Delivery Instruction Slip (DIS) is executed. Meera Associates verifies stamp duty compliance on every deal to ensure an unchallengeable legal audit trail.

Taxation on unlisted shares in India is governed by the Income Tax Act:

  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG): If shares are held for 24 months or less from purchase date, net gains are taxed at your applicable individual income tax slab rates.
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): If shares are held for more than 24 months, net gains are taxed at 12.5% (as per recent Finance Act provisions) without indexation benefits.
  • Securities Transaction Tax (STT): Not applicable on off-market transactions. Stamp duty @ 0.015% is paid at the time of transfer.

Note: Investors are advised to consult their chartered accountant or tax consultant for personalized advice.
